How to choose a web developer you can trust

Buying guide6 min read2026

Hiring the wrong developer is expensive — not only in money, but in wasted months and a site you cannot maintain. Whether you are in Awka, Lagos or anywhere in Nigeria, use this checklist before you pay a deposit.

1. Ask to see relevant work

Portfolio screenshots are good; live links are better. Look for projects similar to yours (business site, store, booking system). Ask what the developer personally built versus what a larger team delivered. Vague answers are a warning sign.

2. Expect a written scope and price

A professional should document what is included, what is not, timeline and payment stages. “We will figure it out as we go” often becomes endless extras and arguments. Fixed or clearly phased pricing protects both sides.

3. Understand the process

Healthy projects usually follow discovery → design or structure → development → review → launch → support. You should know when you will see drafts, how many revision rounds you get, and how feedback is collected. Silence for weeks with no staging link is not normal.

4. Clarify ownership and access

After final payment you should control your domain, hosting and website files or CMS login. Ask who hosts the site, how you update content, and what happens if you stop working together. Avoid arrangements where only the developer can “open the site” forever.

5. Check communication style

Before signing, notice response time and clarity on WhatsApp or email. If explanations are dismissive now, they rarely improve under deadline pressure. You want someone who can translate technical choices into business language.

Red flags

  • Price far below market with “same quality” promises
  • No contract, no scope, full payment upfront only
  • Cannot show any past work or references
  • Guarantees ranking #1 on Google in days
  • Refuses to give you admin or hosting access

Green flags

  • Clear portfolio and realistic timelines
  • Written proposal and milestone payments
  • Staging previews and structured revisions
  • Post-launch support window
  • Training so your team can make basic updates
