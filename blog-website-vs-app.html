Website vs mobile app: which does your business need first?

Strategy6 min read2026

Many business owners ask the same question: “Should we build a website or a mobile app?” The honest answer is that most businesses need a website first. An app becomes valuable when you have a clear, repeated use case that a browser cannot handle as well.

What a website is best at

A website is your public home on the internet. It is easy to share, easy to find on Google, and works on any phone without an install. It is ideal for:

  • Explaining what you sell and who you serve
  • Collecting enquiries and WhatsApp leads
  • Building trust with about, services and contact pages
  • Running a simple or full online store
  • Publishing updates, prices and location details

If customers mainly need to discover you, compare options and contact you, a website is usually the right starting point.

When a mobile app makes sense

Apps shine when people use your product often and need speed, offline access, or phone features such as push notifications, camera or location. Good candidates include:

  • Booking and appointment systems used weekly
  • Loyalty or membership experiences
  • Delivery tracking and driver apps
  • Internal staff tools used every day
  • Products where notifications drive engagement

Apps also cost more to build and maintain (typically from ₦1M+ for a solid cross-platform product), and users must download them. That investment only pays off when usage is frequent.

A simple decision framework

  • Start with a website if you are still validating demand, need SEO, or mainly want enquiries and sales online.
  • Add an app later if customers already use your service regularly and ask for faster access or notifications.
  • Build both only when budget and operations support two products — for example a marketing site plus a logged-in app.

What most Nigerian SMEs should do

For shops, consultants, schools, churches, clinics and local service businesses, a professional website (and sometimes an e-commerce store) delivers more value per naira than jumping straight to an app. Once traffic, sales or bookings are proven, an app can be the next growth layer.
