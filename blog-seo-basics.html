SEO basics for small business websites in Nigeria

SEO5 min read2026

SEO (Search Engine Optimisation) simply means helping Google understand and recommend your website when people search. You do not need to be technical to get the basics right. These steps work for shops, clinics, schools, consultants and local services across Nigeria.

1. Use clear page titles and headings

Every important page should have a unique title that names the service and location when relevant — for example “Wedding photographer in Enugu” or “Auto spare parts store in Awka”. Use one main heading (H1) that matches what the page is about. Avoid vague titles like “Home” or “Welcome”.

2. Write for humans first

Describe what you offer in plain language. Answer the questions customers actually ask: price range, areas you serve, how to book, what makes you different. Keyword stuffing (“best best cheapest lagos abuja…”) looks spammy and rarely ranks well long term.

3. Make the site fast and mobile-friendly

Google prioritises sites that load quickly on phones. Compress images, avoid huge unused scripts, and test your site on a real Android device. If buttons are hard to tap or text is tiny, fix that before chasing advanced SEO tricks.

4. Show your business information clearly

Put your phone number, WhatsApp, email and service area in the footer and contact page. If you have a physical address, list it consistently. Create or claim a Google Business Profile for local searches — many customers find businesses on Maps before they ever type a website URL.

5. Earn simple trust signals

Publish real work examples, testimonials and useful articles (like this one). Share your site on social platforms and ask satisfied clients to mention you online. Links and mentions from real local pages help more than buying fake “SEO packages” that promise overnight rank-1 results.

What not to waste money on

  • Guaranteed #1 ranking promises
  • Thousands of fake backlinks
  • Stuffing every page with the same keywords
  • Ignoring content quality while only “optimising code”
